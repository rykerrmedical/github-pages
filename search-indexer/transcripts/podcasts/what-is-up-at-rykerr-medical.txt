Hey friends and welcome to the Riker Medical Podcast. Riker Medical is a platform for education focused on emergency, transport, and critical care medicine. We believe that the sharing of knowledge should be free, accessible to all, and that innovative use of technology can facilitate that effort. This podcast is just one piece of that whole project. Also, while we may mention medical treatments and interventions along the way, remember that we are just strangers on the internet and don't do things you aren't allowed to do. We'll start with a review of how we got here. We'll explain where things are at and how this podcast fits into the picture. We'll paint a vision of what we hope this project to become and then we'll end it with a discussion about how you can get involved. Riker Medical started with a book on ventilator management for pre-hospital folks. We self-published that resource to help clinicians in the field gain a basic understanding of ventilators and transport. The idea from the beginning was to make that a free resource for everyone. We got set up as an LLC and started the webpage to have a place to host it. You can access and download that book for free. Just head over to RikerMedical.com and navigate to this section on vent management. Once we had the website started, we slowly began to add other bits of content there, various resources for clinicians in the field. We've got some drug references, examples of protocols, cheat sheets, that sort of thing. There's lots more in the works, but it takes time and we got distracted by another idea that brought us to where we are now. The thought struck us that there are lots of people out there working in the field that have insight to offer, but there's not a good way for those motivated individuals to share what they know without a lot of upfront effort to make it happen. Our vent book, for example, has been out for over a year now and the reach we've been able to achieve is pretty limited. That seems unfortunate as we imagine there are great many medics and nurses out there who would be stoked to come across this type of thing. Our solution for that is to try and use this platform, which is slowly gaining attention, to allow others to publicize what they have to offer to the greater community. Taking that idea one step further, we thought it'd be pretty rad to make it happen via a continuing education exchange. We could build out a CE program to give hours back to people who contribute their work. The idea is to foster some sense of community that both provides valuable content and gives a benefit back to those who do the work. Of course, we want the thing to be free. Now if you follow us on social media, you may already know that we did get granted organizational accreditation for a CE program through CAPSIE. That's the Commission on Accreditation for Pre-Hospital Continuing Education. Unfortunately and for a variety of reasons that we don't need to get into, CAPSIE has revoked that accreditation until we add a few more pieces to the puzzle and resubmit our application. That's where we're at for now. This podcast will eventually be part of that CE program. Listeners will be able to pitch ideas to us for episodes. We will work with them to make it happen and then CE credit will be awarded in the exchange. In addition, there will be an option for the community at large to get credit for listening to certain episodes and taking quizzes or some sort of competency assessment that accompany those episodes. For now, we don't have the capacity to award credit, but we are still looking for content contributors to work with us through the process so that we can demonstrate to CAPSIE exactly how the quality control aspects work on our end. In the meantime, we here at Riker Medical have a few ideas for episodes and will try to contribute regularly to the show. Now, it is worth noting that not all of the stuff we host on the show or on the website fall into the CE program. You can use the very same application to get in touch and we'll take it from there. As far as what sorts of things fall into the CE model, that takes a bit of explaining. Our initial pitch to CAPSIE included seven activities. First are the podcast and blog, which are pretty self-explanatory. We'd give credit to folks for contributing episodes or posts. We also envisioned a mentorship activity in which we could give credit to an experienced clinician for building out a mentorship program for someone new to the field or with less experience. Then there was the idea of annotated illustrations, offering credit for explaining concepts visually or graphically. We also had a reflection activity in which someone could share an experience via the platform in an active reflection or debrief, whether that be text, audio, or video. Another idea was the critique, essentially a way for someone to informally peer-reviewed content produced by someone else and to provide constructive feedback on whatever it might be. Lastly, was the instructional design activity in which we give credit to people for building out courses or lessons. The hope moving forward is to find a way to make all of these activities work. The catch is that we now also have to have a mechanism to offer the community at large the opportunity to earn credit for participating in or consuming whatever content we put out through the CE program. Just to be clear, this is at the request of CAPSIE as the accrediting body, so there's no real workaround for us there. The plan is to get content up on a learning management system or LMS so that anyone can access those final projects and participate in them for credit, which is cool. That means that we'll eventually have a library of stuff to meet whatever CE needs you might have. The downside or the problem to be overcome is that not all of these activities we initially envisioned are readily testable. So let's say you want to do a reflection on a run you had and how it affected you personally. That's not something that lends itself to educational goals or learning outcomes that we could put down on a lesson plan for others to get credit from, which is totally okay and we're still down to host your story. It just means that we've got to sort out which contributions will be part of the CE program and which will be just for the sake of getting it out there into the universe and sharing the experience. Another problematic example is the mentorship program. It's a bit tough to test outsiders on a one-on-one mentorship session. That said, we can work to design those plans to focus on specific goals or objectives. We could then record those sessions and share via the LMS like any other asynchronous course. In fact, there's also the option to live stream them so that participants can get credit in real time. In that scenario, we'd be able to give credit to the mentor for developing and guiding the session, to the live audience for participating synchronously, and then to the stragglers who watch it asynchronously via the LMS. We've been toying with this idea along with some of our partners, so more on that whenever we get it worked out.
